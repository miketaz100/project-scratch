Circle-and-Line Drive

Two small motors, each turning a crank with an offset pin, stacked one on top of the other. Neither motor ever reverses. How fast and which way each one turns decides the path the pad traces, so the same hardware scratches in circles or in straight lines, and firmware can switch between them at any moment.

Crank 1 (lower motor) Crank 2 (upper motor) Path of the pad

The small square is the pad. It slides without rotating, carried by the tip of crank 2. In line mode, the nudge button turns the scratch direction by half the nudge, here 30°. In circle mode, the size button changes the phase between the cranks, which shrinks or grows the circle.

Why two cranks can draw either shape

Each crank is a pin spinning on a circle 7.5 mm in radius. The pad sits at the end of the second crank, so its position is the sum of the two spinning pins. When both spin the same way they stay lined up and add into one bigger circle, up to 15 mm in radius. When they spin opposite ways, their sideways movements cancel and their movements along one direction add, so the pad runs back and forth along a straight 30 mm line. The direction of that line is set by where the two cranks meet, so shifting one motor shifts the line.

Cost of the flexibility, by the pad agent's estimate: about 20 g and 15 mm of height over a fixed straight-line gear, two small encoder motors, and a printed coupling between the stages. The line stays straight as long as the two crank radii match within about 0.1 mm; a small mismatch just opens it into a very thin ellipse.
