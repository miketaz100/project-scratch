# LEAP ROUND 3 — brief (common to all agents)

## Michael's review of round 2 (2026-10-02)
1. HELMET IS REQUIRED. Head-worn, not a cradle or pillow. (Round 2 agents A and E both preferred a cradle; that is overruled. Their geometry ideas, e.g. a bail centred on the skull with its pivot axis through the ears, may be transplanted onto a helmet.)
2. CIRCLE AND LINE BOTH. Michael likes the circle and wants the straight rake too, selectable. The Director's answer: a two-stage path synthesiser (leap2-B Leap 4): two stacked parallel-crank eccentrics r1 = r2 ≈ 7.5 mm, upper stage fed through an Oldham coupling, two one-way motors with encoders; same direction = circle (size via phase), opposite = 30 mm line, opposite with ε speed difference = precessing line, phase step = heading jump. The all-air three-bellows synchro (leap2-C C1) can also make circle or line by phase and is an alternative.

## The architecture so far (binding unless your leap beats it with numbers)
- Air-piston pins: force = pressure × area (seating-independent), relief-valve cap, vent = lift, valves in a desk box (SMC S070-class, not BP-monitor valves), hover-and-bite landing (leap2-F L3), omni nail on a trailing neck (leap2-B L2), palm skids keeping the pad within ~5° of scalp normal (leap2-A L2).
- A small pad (≈ 8–16 pins, ≈ 60–85 mm across) that scrubs (circle or line, selectable) and travels slowly over the head (2–5 cm/s drift, up to 80 mm/s for sweeps per leap2-D).
- Pattern layer: κ-grammar + fatigue ledger (leap2-D).
- All of it on a HELMET.
