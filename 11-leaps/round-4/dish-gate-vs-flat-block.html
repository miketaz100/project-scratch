Dish Gate vs Flat Block

Both pads scrub the same 30 mm line across the same curved scalp. The difference is what the pin block slides on. The flat block slides in a straight line, so the scalp curves away beneath its outer pins, and every pin needs its own valve to reach down and lift up at the right moment. The dish-gate block slides along a curved seat that matches the scalp, so the pins stay at the same height above the skin. The seat's raised rim lifts every nail at the stroke ends by shape alone.

Before

Flat block, a valve per pin

  • The block slides straight, so the outer pins hang 4 to 8 mm higher at one end of the stroke than the other.
  • Each pin has its own valve, tube and sensor so firmware can drop it down to the skin and lift it again on time.
  • Between bites, pins hover 5 mm above the hair on friction collars.
After

Dish gate, the shape does the timing

  • The block rides on three small domes along a curved seat, the dashed blue line, centred on the scalp's own curve, so every pin keeps within 1 to 3 mm of the skin.
  • Near each end of the stroke the seat rises, which lifts the block and every nail clear. No valve does it.
  • All pins share one or two air lines at constant pressure, so each still presses with a set force.
Cross-section through the pad, crown of the head (radius 85 mm). Three of the six pins are shown. Heights are exaggerated 2× so the gaps are visible.

What it gives up: firmware can no longer choose individual pins or the exact order they land in. The two groups can still be switched and set to different forces. The slight differences in how far each pin sits from the skin make them land a few hundredths of a second apart on their own. The longest single stroke stays about 23 mm; longer sweeps come from moving the pad.

Open safety question. If a nail catches a hair, the dish gate lifts it with about 0.6 N, while the safety review's fix assumed a nail with 12 mm of free play could slip away more gently. The agent proposes giving the block 12 mm of free vertical travel and a stop-and-vent reflex, but the safety red team has to rule on it before the dish gate is adopted.
