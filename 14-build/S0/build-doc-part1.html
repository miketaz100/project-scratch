Scratch Build Part 1: Does It Feel Good?

The cheap first test. You build two small hand-driven versions of the scratching pad, try them on a ball, then a helper tries them on your head. If they feel like a good scratch, you buy the next round of parts. If not, you've spent about $127 and we fix the nail before building anything bigger.

What you need: about $127 in parts and printing if you have a 3D printer, one helper for a 30-minute session at the end, and about 12–14 hours spread over a long weekend or a few evenings.
  • No printer? A Bambu Lab A1 mini is about $219 on sale and is worth it, because the later stages print a lot. Or order prints from a service for about $110–190.
  • Two benches: "V1" is built exactly to the original spec. "V2" is the corrected version, with longer, gentler strokes. The test tells us which one to build for real.

Safety first

Stop and lift straight up (never drag sideways) if there is any pain, sting, tug, catch, heat, a missing nail, anything loose, or the bench rocks on your head.
  • Glasses on for both of you during the head session, and at the bench when working with springs, magnets or cut wire.
  • The helper never presses down. The bench rests on your head by its own weight.
  • Count the nails before and after every bout: 3 in, 3 out. A missing nail is in your hair: stop and find it.
  • Crown only, at least 3 cm from your hairline, ears and eyes. Clean, dry, combed hair. Not on irritated or sunburnt skin.
  • Magnets are a swallowing hazard. Keep the bag sealed, away from children and pets. Count them out and back.
  • Superglue: gloves and a damp cloth nearby.
  • At most 20 minutes of head contact per day. Any redness should be gone 10 minutes later.

Helper: how to run each bout

"Eight short bouts, thirty seconds each, short rests between. Keep your eyes closed. After each one I'll ask seven quick questions. Say stop or lift your hand any time and I'll lift it straight off."

  • V1 or V2 bench: rest it on the crown by its own weight, steady it with one hand on the post on top, never pressing. With the other hand push the round grip along the grooves at about one stroke per second: 8 strokes on one line, then move to the next line, through all four.
  • Rake: hold the handle, skids on the crown. Stroke about 2.5 cm back and forth, one per second, lifting about 5 mm at each end. Turn 45° every 8 strokes. Never press.
  • Fingers: a normal friendly scratch with short clean nails, same pace, same patch.
  • At 30 seconds, lift straight off, ask the seven questions, count the nails, rest 30–60 seconds.
