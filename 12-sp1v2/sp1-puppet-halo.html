SP1 Puppet Halo

A light skeleton helmet carries one hand-sized pad of 12 air-driven nails. The pad drifts over your scalp on a carbon arc that pivots at your ears, and scrubs in a slowly turning line, a straight line or a circle. All the motors, pumps and valves sit in a portable box nearby; only air lines and a thin cable reach the helmet.

Status: first full draft, under review. This is the spec as the system architect wrote it. Three red teams have since found fixes, and the pin count may drop from 12 to make the second pad fit under the weight limit. A decision analysis is running now; this page will be updated once you choose a direction.

Key numbers

ItemValue
Nails12, on an 18 mm grid; force 0.08 to 0.50 N each, capped by a relief valve at 0.96 N
Nail tipplastic cone with a 2 mm flat and 0.4 mm edge radius, on a thin flexible wire neck
Scrub modesslowly turning 30 mm line (default, 1.4 strokes per second, 4.5° per stroke), straight line, circle up to 15 mm radius
Travelfront to back and side to side over the scalp; slow drift 2 to 5 cm/s, sweeps up to 8 cm/s
Weight on headabout 350 to 400 g with one pad; about 575 g with two, over the 500 g limit
Drive box30 × 22 × 11 cm, under 2.5 kg, certified 12 V adapter
Connectionone 1.4 m bundle about 17 mm thick; latched at the box, magnetic breakaway at the helmet
Your controlshold-to-run lever with a squeeze egg ("there", "go that way", hard squeeze to stop), plus an emergency-stop button
Costabout $1,240 as drafted; the buildability red team puts it at $1,410 to $1,640 with its fixes, plus tools
