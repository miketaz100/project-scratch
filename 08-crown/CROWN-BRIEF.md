# RESTING CROWN — concept brief (common to all concept teams)

Context: Michael challenged the monitor-arm frame as too expensive and too different from the eventual head-worn product. The Director's response: the HAND MODULE (three nails at 24 mm pitch on feeler-stock leaves, enclosed palm, knuckle plate, TM1 tips, XL330 stroke servo on an 80 mm pendulum arm/yoke, radial MGN9 float with a small dead weight, geometric lift-off at both stroke ends) stays exactly as engineered. What changes is what HOLDS it. The new idea: a light structure that simply RESTS on the skull like a hat, with the hand floating inside on its own dead weight, so gravity sets the nail force and the structure's seating slop does not matter. Hands-free, nothing clamped to furniture, head-worn in form.

Read before designing: 05-engineering/DESIGN-FREEZE.md §1 items 5–8, 05-engineering/DESIGN-FREEZE-ADDENDUM-1.md, 05-engineering/mechanical.md §2 (dimensions), §6 (float), §7–8 (wrist, hand), §5.3 (servo), 01-foundations/safety-requirements.md (red lines, esp. 6, 8, 10: head-borne ≤ 500 g, releasable one-handed in ≤ 3 s, no non-breakaway chin strap), 01-foundations/hair-interaction.md §6 (machine rules), 03-tournament/judge-2-engineering.md §0 F1/F3/F5 (why head-worn lost: force reference, mass, fail-safe), 04-redteam/redteam-2-mechanical.md §2 and §8 (flinch, fail-safe).

The hand module as it exists: yoke (two cheeks 5 mm PETG at Y ±93–98, crossbar at X +45–57, rail mast), XL330 at Y −101 to −127 (horn +Y), idler at Y +101–111, elbow axis at Z +84 above the scalp apex, rail 100 mm radial, carriage + wrist + palm floating ≈ 90 g bare + 0–100 g slugs, palm 52 × 169 × 33 mm, knuckle plate 64 × 90 mm R 90 underside, nails at Z 0, stroke ±25°, swept envelope within R 91 mm of the elbow axis. Fixed-frame interface: the elbow carrier (servo cradle at −Y, idler housing at +Y) must be held rigidly relative to the skull at Z +84, with the 270 mm carrier beam or an equivalent. You may shorten/re-route the carrier (servo and idler can move inboard to |Y| ≈ 95 if the yoke is narrowed; state what changes).

Your concept must answer, with numbers:
1. GEOMETRY: side (XZ) and front (YZ) views as inline SVG with dimensions; where the structure touches the head (pads: count, size, location, material), how it clears the hair canopy (≥ 3 mm gaps or closed, drafted, no rotation within 30 mm of scalp), and the module's position.
2. FORCE REFERENCE: show that nail force = floating dead weight (± leaf preload differences) regardless of ±3–5 mm seating variation; the float travel required; what happens when the head tilts (force ∝ cos tilt; tangential component along the rail is bounded by the rail; state the tilt limit).
3. STABILITY: the XL330 reaction torque (≈ 0.1 N·m peak, 1–3 Hz) and the hand's drag (≤ 1.5 N) try to twist/slide the structure on the skull; compute the holding moment from pad friction (µ ≈ 0.3–0.5 on hair/skin) × normal load × lever arms and state whether a loose retaining band is needed, its tension, and that it is breakaway/elastic (red line 10).
4. MASS: budget every part; total on head ≤ 500 g (target ≤ 350 g including the module ≈ 150–200 g); centre of mass and how the pads share the load; comfort over 20 min (pad pressure ≤ 5 kPa).
5. COVERAGE: which regions it reaches (crown only? crown + upper occiput? repositionable?), how region/direction change happens (lift and reseat by hand; detents), and whether the back of the head is reachable leaning forward.
6. SAFETY: donning/doffing one-handed ≤ 3 s; what happens on power loss (hand rests at dead weight: is that acceptable here, since the user lifts the whole thing off? argue it against red line 8 and red team 2's flinch analysis); e-stop + hold-to-run retained; snag reflex; flinch/head jerk ±20 mm; nothing within 25 mm of ears/eyes; cable routing.
7. NOISE: bone-conduction path from the servo through pads; mitigation (TPU isolators, servo off-head via a short cable is NOT allowed here — the servo stays in the yoke).
8. BUILD: printed parts list (name, size, qty, material) and bought parts with cost delta vs the hand module alone; build hours; what is reusable from the existing cad/ files.
9. RISKS: top five, each with the bench test that retires it (mannequin head + servo running; tilt test; drag test).
10. A one-paragraph honest verdict: is this better than the monitor-arm stand for SP1, and what would make you choose the stand after all?

## Director note on the chin strap (added after Michael's question)
A chin strap IS permitted and probably expected. Its job here is retention only (stop sliding/twisting under servo reaction and drag), never the force reference, because the nail force comes from the floating dead weight. Requirements: breakaway or elastic (magnetic buckle such as a Fidlock-type, or an elastic strap that stretches off; red line 10 forbids a non-breakaway chin strap), one-handed release ≤ 3 s, tension low enough that jaw movement is comfortable (quantify: strap tension needed vs the holding moment you compute in item 3), and routed clear of the ears. Analyse how jaw motion (talking, swallowing, ≈ 2–5 mm strap excursion) moves the crown and show the float absorbs it.
